About

I work on data and AI systems that have to hold up in the real world: where the data is heterogeneous, the ground truth is partial or missing, and someone will act on the output.

I recently joined TU Wien as a postdoctoral researcher, after five years at Politecnico di Milano. There I worked on the PRIN 2022 project S-PIC4CHU, studying how choices made during data selection, transformation and curation propagate into the behaviour of the analytical and AI systems built on top of them. Provenance, quality checks and fairness are not add-ons at the end of that pipeline — they are decided much earlier.

I completed my PhD in Information Technology at Politecnico di Milano in 2025, supervised by Letizia Tanca, with a thesis on urban analysis frameworks spanning data modelling, geospatial clustering and sequential group recommendation. Along the way I have worked on workplace-safety data with INAIL, industrial fault detection, graph models for medical imaging metadata, and text and network analysis of anti-corruption reports at Bocconi.

What ties this together is the pairing of technical methods with a human-centred reading of them: systems that are sound, interpretable, and that still make sense to the people whose domain they describe. In 2024 I spent six months at Tampere University with Konstantinos Stefanidis working on diversity and fairness in group recommenders, a collaboration that continues.

Research

Fairness and diversity in recommendation
When a system recommends to a group over a sequence of rounds, fairness cannot be judged one round at a time. ADAPT addresses how satisfaction and diversity can be balanced across a sequence, and how to evaluate that balance when no single correct ranking exists.
Semantics-aware data preparation and provenance
Data preparation is where most of the bias and most of the silent errors enter a pipeline. Within S-PIC4CHU I work on semantics-enriched preparation techniques and provenance-aware quality control, so that the transformations behind a result stay inspectable and accountable.
LLM-based ranking and retrieval
Language models are increasingly used to rank and retrieve, including over structured data, but their judgements are hard to evaluate. My current work looks at semantic ranking over tables and at diversification in the retrieval step of RAG pipelines, with evaluation designed for settings without a reference ordering.
Data modelling for applied and policy-oriented settings
Conceptual and graph-based models for domains where the data was never collected with analysis in mind: urban form and mobility, SDG 11 indicators, workplace incident reports, MRI metadata, production-line defects. The modelling choice usually determines what questions can be asked at all.
